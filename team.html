Meet Our Team

The people behind every Magic Tools Contracting project.

Magic Tools Contracting is a small, hands-on crew — no subcontractors, no middlemen. Here's who you'll actually see on site, plus the contracts support that keeps every job properly documented.

Placeholder team bios — to be replaced with real photos and finalized details for each team member.

LS

Luciano da Silva

Owner & Lead Mason

Luciano founded Magic Tools Masonry after years of working brick, block, and stone jobs across the Greater Boston area, and built the company's reputation one patio, one chimney, and one retaining wall at a time. He still shows up to most job sites personally, walks every estimate with the homeowner before a crew is scheduled, and signs off on the final punch list before calling a project done. As the company grew from pure masonry into general contracting, painting, and decks, Luciano stayed the one constant — the standard every new hire and every subcontractor is measured against. Clients who've worked with him more than once tend to ask for him by name on the next project, which is exactly the kind of word-of-mouth the business was built on in the first place.

TO

Thiago Oliveira

Carpentry & Decks Lead

Thiago leads the carpentry side of the business, overseeing deck builds from the first post hole to the final coat of stain. He came up through residential framing crews before specializing in outdoor living spaces, and he's the one who walks homeowners through layout decisions — railing styles, decking material, how a deck ties into an existing patio or retaining wall — before a single board gets cut. He's particular about letting lumber acclimate before installation and about flashing details that keep a deck from becoming a water problem five years down the line, two things that don't show up in a photo but matter a lot once the New England weather sets in. Homeowners tend to remember him as the guy who double-checked the span tables before anyone else even thought to ask.

RS

Rafael Souza

Mason & Concrete Specialist

Rafael handles much of the day-to-day masonry and concrete work — steps, walkways, patios, and the repointing jobs that keep older Arlington-area foundations and chimneys sound. He trained originally as a bricklayer and has since picked up concrete finishing, stone veneer, and basic waterproofing along the way, which makes him one of the few people on the crew comfortable moving between a historic brick repair and a brand-new poured patio in the same week. He's known for being meticulous about grade and drainage before the first batch of concrete ever gets mixed, on the theory that almost every masonry callback traces back to water that was never given anywhere to go. On a crew that prides itself on craftsmanship, Rafael is often the one holding everyone else to that standard.

GC

Gabriel Costa

Painting & Finishing Lead

Gabriel runs the painting, staining, and finishing work that rounds out a project after the masonry and carpentry are done — interior and exterior painting, deck and fence staining, and tile work in kitchens and bathrooms. He came into the trade through finish carpentry and cabinetry before focusing on paint and coatings specifically, which gives him an eye for the small prep details — caulking, sanding, proper primer for the substrate — that end up being the difference between a finish that looks good at handoff and one that still looks good two winters later. He's also usually the last person on site before a homeowner gets their space back, so he tends to be the one doing the final walkthrough and pointing out touch-ups before anyone else even notices them.

MA

Mariana Alves

Contracts & Legal Advisor

Mariana isn't on job sites, but she's involved in nearly every project before the first shovel goes into the ground — reviewing homeowner contracts, lien waivers, and permit paperwork so that every job is properly documented on both sides. She works with Magic Tools Contracting on an advisory basis, helping make sure estimates, change orders, and payment schedules are written in plain language that both the crew and the homeowner understand, rather than boilerplate nobody reads. For homeowners, that mostly means contracts that are clear about scope, timeline, and price up front, with fewer surprises if a project's scope changes partway through. It's not the most visible part of the business, but it's part of why clients can trust the paperwork as much as the craftsmanship.
